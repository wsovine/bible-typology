## Type: The Synagogue (Old Covenant Assembly)  (`the-synagogue-old-covenant-assembly`, applies to type)

- **Description:** The synagogue/assembly of the Old Covenant which rejected the Messiah; contrasted with the Church that received him as head.
- **Link note, The Synagogue (Old Covenant Assembly) → The One True Church:** St. Augustine: 'The synagogue rejected the Messias, but God received him, and made him head of the Church.'

## Type: Destruction of Jerusalem as Divine Judgment  (`destruction-of-jerusalem-as-divine-judgment`, applies to type)

- **Link note, Destruction of Jerusalem as Divine Judgment → Final Judgment:** Worthington: the ruin of Jerusalem by Titus 'is a figure of the world's destruction, and of the eternal punishment of the wicked.'
- **Link note, The Babylonian Exile → Destruction of Jerusalem as Divine Judgment:** Origen/St. Jerome: the exile 'was only a figure of what they endured since they rejected Christ.'

## Type: The Babylonian Exile  (`the-babylonian-exile`, applies to type)

- **Description:** Israel's exile, deprived of religion, temple, and princes; a figure of the long desolation the Jews endured after rejecting Christ.
- **Link note, The Babylonian Exile → Destruction of Jerusalem as Divine Judgment:** Origen/St. Jerome: the exile 'was only a figure of what they endured since they rejected Christ.'

**Clarification:** Some Fathers read the centuries of Jewish suffering after AD 70 as the result of rejecting Christ. The Church does not. Responsibility for the Passion cannot be extended “to other Jews of different times and places,” and the Catechism notes that Christians have “all too often burdened the Jews alone” with it.

- Catechism §597: “Still less can we extend responsibility to other Jews of different times and places”
- Catechism §598: “a responsibility with which they have all too often burdened the Jews alone”

## Link: The Vineyard of Israel → The Gospel (New Covenant)  (applies to link-note)

- **Link note:** Ven. Bede: the vine represents the Synagogue; Christ declares it no longer acceptable 'since the figures were not to pass into reality' — the New Covenant.

## Type: Heretics  (`heretics`, applies to type)

- **Description:** Those who teach false doctrine and oppose the true faith within the Christian era.
- **Link note, Ishmael → Heretics:** St. Augustine: Ishmael/Hagar are 'a figure of heretics (who are the children of the bond-woman) unjustly persecuting the Catholic Church.'
- **Link note, Sanaballat → Heretics:** Ven. Bede/Worthington: Sanaballat 'was a persecutor, and prefigured heretics.'

**Clarification:** In Catholic teaching, heresy is a precise term: “the obstinate post-baptismal denial of some truth which must be believed with divine and catholic faith.” It is not a name for other Christians today. Those raised in separated communities “with good reason are accepted as brothers in the Lord by the children of the Catholic Church.”

- Catechism §2089: “the obstinate post-baptismal denial of some truth which must be believed with divine and catholic faith”
- Catechism §818: “with good reason are accepted as brothers in the Lord by the children of the Catholic Church”

## Link: Job → The One True Church  (applies to link-note)

- **Link note:** St. Gregory (via Worthington): Job 'was a figure of the Church, defending the common cause; while his friends, like heretics, speak both true and false things.'

**Clarification:** St. Gregory wrote this in the sixth century, long before today's divisions among Christians. Of the Christian communities separated from her, the Church teaches that “many elements of sanctification and of truth” are found beyond her visible bounds, and “all these blessings come from Christ and lead to him.”

- Catechism §819: “many elements of sanctification and of truth”
- Catechism §819: “All these blessings come from Christ and lead to him”

## Type: The Reprobate (the Rejected)  (`the-reprobate-the-rejected`, applies to type)

- **Description:** Those rejected by God and excluded from salvation, in contrast to the elect.
- **Link note, Esau → The Reprobate (the Rejected):** St. Augustine: Esau 'was a figure of the reprobate, though not of course one himself.'

**Clarification:** “Reprobate” is an old theological word for those who are finally lost. The Church teaches that no one is rejected in advance: “God predestines no one to go to hell; for this, a willful turning away from God (a mortal sin) is necessary, and persistence in it until the end.”

- Catechism §1037: “God predestines no one to go to hell; for this, a willful turning away from God (a mortal sin) is necessary, and persistence in it until the end”

## Type: Esau  (`esau`, applies to type)

- **Description:** Esau, who lost his birthright and privileges to Jacob; a figure of the reprobate, though not himself necessarily reprobate.
- **Link note, Esau → The Reprobate (the Rejected):** St. Augustine: Esau 'was a figure of the reprobate, though not of course one himself.'

**Clarification:** As the commentators themselves say, Esau is a figure here, not a verdict on the man. The Church teaches that “Christ died for all men without exception” and that God “desires all men to be saved.”

- Catechism §605: “Christ died for all men without exception”
- Catechism §1058: “desires all men to be saved”

## Link: The Virgin of Emmanuel (Isaiah 7:14) → Mary  (applies to link-note)

- **Link note:** St. Jerome (on 'almah' = virgin) and the Fathers 'generally apply all to Christ'; the virgin sign is fulfilled in Mary.

**Clarification:** Strictly speaking, Isaiah 7:14 is a prophecy rather than a type: a promise spoken in words, not a person or event that foreshadows. The Catechism treats it as a promise kept: of Jesus' virginal conception it says, “The Church sees here the fulfillment of the divine promise given through the prophet Isaiah.”

- Catechism §497: “The Church sees here the fulfillment of the divine promise given through the prophet Isaiah”
